Planetary Surface Mission Testing

Identifying Earth environments analogous to permanent Moon base locations and Mars.

Before landing astronauts or autonomous rovers on extraterrestrial regolith, mobility systems, drilling rigs, and habitat life support must be verified in terrestrial environments that physically replicate lunar and Martian surface conditions.

NASA Challenge Objective

"Identify Earth locations that are analogous to permanent Moon base locations and Mars."

EARTHY addresses this challenge through calibrated satellite datasets (Copernicus DEM, LRO LOLA, MGS MOLA, MERRA-2, MODIS), Zero-Veto biophysical gating, 1st Wasserstein distribution metrics, and a decoupled Dual-Score framework separating hardware analogy from permanent settlement suitability.

Mathematical & Algorithmic Formulations

2nd-Order Central Difference Slope Gradient

∂z/∂x ≈ (z[i, j+1] - z[i, j-1]) / (2·Δx)
∂z/∂y ≈ (z[i+1, j] - z[i-1, j]) / (2·Δy)
θ_slope = arctan(√( (∂z/∂x)² + (∂z/∂y)² )) · (180 / π)

1st Wasserstein Distance on Slope Distributions

W₁(u, v) = ∫ |U(t) - V(t)| dt
Measures the minimum work (in degrees of angular transport) required to morph the Earth slope CDF into the planetary target CDF.
